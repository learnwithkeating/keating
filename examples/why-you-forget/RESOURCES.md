# Why You Forget Resources

Twelve sources, one per load-bearing claim in the lessons. Every number quoted in a lesson
comes from one of these, and each entry says which lesson leans on it, so a claim you doubt
can be traced in one step.

## Knowledge

- [Roediger, H. L., & Karpicke, J. D. (2006). _Psychological Science_, 17(3), 249–255.](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x)
  The crossover experiment: repeated study 83% at five minutes and 40% at one week, repeated testing 71% and 61%. Use for: the claim that the ranking of study methods depends on when you measure. Lessons 1 and 3.
- [Soderstrom, N. C., & Bjork, R. A. (2015). _Perspectives on Psychological Science_, 10(2), 176–199.](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691615569000)
  The modern statement of the learning-versus-performance distinction, and the reachable entry point to Bjork & Bjork's new theory of disuse (Bjork, R. A., & Bjork, E. L., 1992, in _From Learning Processes to Cognitive Processes_, Vol. 2, pp. 35–67, Erlbaum, which has no open link). Use for: storage strength versus retrieval strength, and why practice performance misleads. Remember it is a framework, not one measured effect. Lessons 1 and 2.
- [Rhodes, M. G., & Tauber, S. K. (2011). _Psychological Bulletin_, 137(1), 131–148.](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0021705)
  Delayed judgments of learning are far more accurate than immediate ones, g = 0.93 across 112 effect sizes. Use for: the one change of habit that fixes miscalibrated self-assessment. Lesson 2.
- [Dunlosky, J., Rawson, K. A., Marsh, E. J., Nathan, M. J., & Willingham, D. T. (2013). _Psychological Science in the Public Interest_, 14(1), 4–58.](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266)
  Ten common study techniques rated for utility, with practice testing and distributed practice highest and rereading, highlighting and summarization lowest. Use for: the utility tiers, and for the qualification that low utility means low generality of benefit rather than proven harm. Lessons 2 and 3.
- [Rowland, C. A. (2014). _Psychological Bulletin_, 140(6), 1432–1463.](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037559)
  The testing effect against restudy, g = 0.50 with an interval of 0.42 to 0.58 across 61 studies, plus the moderators including recall versus recognition format. Its success-band moderator values are correlational and unverified against the source table, so treat those as descriptive only. Use for: the headline effect size and the format comparison. Lesson 3.
- [Yang, C., Luo, L., Vadillo, M. A., Yu, R., & Shanks, D. R. (2021). _Psychological Bulletin_, 147(4), 399–435.](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000309)
  The classroom-scale replication, g = 0.499 across 222 studies and 48,478 students. Use for: showing that the laboratory number survives contact with real courses. Lesson 3.
- [Karpicke, J. D., & Roediger, H. L. (2008). _Science_, 319(5865), 966–968.](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1152408)
  Dropping items from testing after one correct recall collapsed retention to about 36% at one week against about 80%; also the source of the finding that learners' performance predictions were uncorrelated with their actual recall. Use for: why one correct answer is not learning, and for the calibration failure in Lesson 2. Lessons 2 and 3.
- [Cepeda, N. J., Pashler, H., Vul, E., Wixted, J. T., & Rohrer, D. (2006). _Psychological Bulletin_, 132(3), 354–380.](https://augmentingcognition.com/assets/Cepeda2006.pdf)
  Distributed practice beat massed practice in 259 of 271 direct comparisons at equal total study time, 47.3% against 36.7% recall. Open access. Use for: the spacing effect itself, and for the equal-time design that makes it non-trivial. Lesson 4.
- [Cepeda, N. J., Vul, E., Rohrer, D., Wixted, J. T., & Pashler, H. (2008). _Psychological Science_, 19(11), 1095–1102.](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED505660.pdf)
  The temporal ridgeline experiment, 1,354 participants: optimal gaps of 1, 11, 21 and 21 days for retention intervals of 7, 35, 70 and 350 days, +64% over massed, d = 1.1. Open access. Use for: sizing a gap to a horizon, and for the asymmetry that says to err longer. Lesson 4.
- [Latimier, A., Peyre, H., & Ramus, F. (2021). _Educational Psychology Review_, 33, 959–987.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-020-09572-8)
  Expanding schedules perform no better than uniform spacing, g = 0.034 and not significant, while spaced retrieval beats massed retrieval at g = 0.74. Use for: retiring the one-three-nine folklore without retiring spacing. Lesson 4.
- [Brunmair, M., & Richter, T. (2019). _Psychological Bulletin_, 145(11), 1029–1052.](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000209)
  Interleaving at g = 0.42 overall, ranging from 0.67 for visually confusable categories and 0.34 for mathematics to null for expository texts and −0.39 for unrelated word-learning materials, where blocking wins. Use for: the boundary, and as a model of reading a meta-analysis by its moderators rather than its average. Lesson 5.
- [Rohrer, D., Dedrick, R. F., Hartwig, M. K., & Cheung, C.-N. (2020). _Journal of Educational Psychology_, 112(1), 40–52.](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2019-rohrer.pdf)
  The preregistered 787-student classroom trial: 61% against 38%, d = 0.83, on a surprise test a month later. Open access. Use for: the strongest single piece of interleaving evidence, in preference to the smaller lab studies. Lesson 5.

## Wisdom (Communities)

There is no forum that can tell you whether this course worked, and that is not a gap in the
listing. Every technique here is defined by a delayed, unassisted measurement, so the only
validating community is one that can put you in front of that measurement.

- **Your own material, one week later, closed book.** The real test of this subject is
  whether you can apply it to something you actually need to know. Pick material you are
  currently studying, use it, then measure at a delay. Use for: the only evidence that
  counts, and the one another person cannot supply for you.
- **A study partner willing to cover the page.** A second person who asks you to produce
  content rather than recognise it converts a review session into a retrieval event, and
  removes your ability to grade your own fluency as understanding. Use for: making the
  attempt genuinely closed book, and for catching the answers you thought you had.
- **The primary sources above, read against the summaries you meet elsewhere.** Almost all
  popular writing on this material drops the boundary conditions: the short-delay reversal,
  the feedback requirement, the interleaving sign flip. Checking a claim against the source
  is itself the discriminating practice this course is about. Use for: calibrating how much
  to trust the next study-tips article you read.

## Gaps

- **No vetted community identified.** No forum, subreddit or local group has been checked
  against the high-trust bar for this topic. The field's practitioner-facing writing is
  uneven, and an unvetted community here would mostly relay the flattened claims the sources
  above contradict. Revisit if a moderated, citation-checking venue turns up.
- **Unit 3 has no lessons yet.** What the evidence does not support is declared as a unit and
  left empty on purpose: the learning-styles literature, the disfluent-font null, and the
  replication failures each need their own sourcing pass before anything is written.
- **Nothing here covers novel multi-step procedures.** Every lesson in this course is
  evidenced on facts, concepts and prose. Whether retrieval practice carries to complex
  problem solving learned from worked examples is genuinely disputed in the literature, and
  no source here settles it.
